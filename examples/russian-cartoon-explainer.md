# Russian Cartoon Explainer — Creative Direction

Use this brief with OpenReels via `--direction examples/russian-cartoon-explainer.md`.

## Language and audience
- Write the complete narration in natural, conversational Russian.
- Use idiomatic Russian, correct grammar, and short sentences suitable for spoken delivery.
- Keep on-screen text in Russian. Avoid transliteration unless a proper name requires it.
- Explain unfamiliar terms simply. Do not invent facts, quotations, dates, or statistics.

## Visual identity
- Target look: polished 2D cartoon educational explainer / animated infographic.
- Use clean hand-drawn shapes, expressive but restrained characters, readable silhouettes, warm friendly colors, and clear visual hierarchy.
- Combine character-led scenes with simple diagrams, labels, maps, icons, and visual metaphors.
- Prefer flat 2D illustration with subtle paper texture and gentle depth; avoid photorealism, 3D game renders, anime tropes, and generic corporate stock-photo aesthetics.
- Use a consistent palette and line weight across all scenes.

## Character continuity
- If a recurring presenter or character is useful, define one canonical appearance in the first scene: age range, face shape, hair, clothing, accessories, palette, and proportions.
- Repeat that exact description in every scene where the character appears. Do not change clothing, hairstyle, colors, or proportions between shots.
- Prefer a small recurring cast. Do not introduce a new character unless the story needs one.
- Treat this brief as creative guidance; image-model consistency is not guaranteed without reference-image conditioning.

## Scene direction
- Build a clear beginning, middle, and ending.
- Each scene should communicate one main idea and have one obvious focal point.
- Use visual metaphors and simple motion: pop-ins, pans, gentle zooms, parallax, arrows, highlights, counters, and diagram reveals.
- Vary shot composition while preserving the same art direction.
- Keep backgrounds uncluttered and reserve negative space for captions.
- Avoid tiny labels, dense paragraphs, illegible pseudo-text, logos, watermarks, and decorative elements that compete with the narration.

## Editing and captions
- Make cuts and transitions follow the meaning and rhythm of the narration.
- Captions must match the Russian voiceover, be concise, and remain inside mobile-safe margins.
- Keep key words visually emphasized without covering faces or important diagrams.
- Use subtle background music below the narration; narration must remain clearly intelligible.
- End with a visual summary or memorable final image, not an abrupt cut.

## Quality checklist
- Every scene supports the spoken sentence.
- Russian spelling and punctuation are correct.
- The same character design and visual language persist throughout.
- No scene contains gibberish text or malformed diagrams.
- The final sequence has a coherent visual rhythm and a clear ending.
